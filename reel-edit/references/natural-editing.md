## All 15 rules from the overview: adoption audit

The overview clip listed below shows these exact headings at 2.6-7.2s. Only five have separate
detailed lesson clips in the supplied folder. For the remaining headings, the operating guidance
below is our conservative interpretation, not a claim that an unseen tutorial specified it.

| # | Visible rule | Adopted behavior / deliberate adaptation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Captions are for reading | Stationary white phrases, no bounce/highlight. The detailed clip suggests 2-3 words; the requested default is **3-4**, using configured fonts (Tajawal/Inter by default). |
| 2 | Zero dead space | Trim idle take starts/ends and genuine retakes. Preserve short internal pauses and complete sentences; literal zero silence would conflict with the user's cutting rules. |
| 3 | Tone decides the gap | Keep a pause that carries thought, emotion, or emphasis. Do not invent a universal gap length or shorten deliberate slow speech. |
| 4 | Tight, never choppy | Review joins in context. No clipped syllables, incomplete words, or jump cuts inside a sentence merely to save time. |
| 5 | Cut words, not just air | Remove duplicate takes and false starts. Do not silently remove unique claims or restructure the speaker's meaning. The user's intact-sentence rule takes precedence over aggressive reference trimming. |
| 6 | Money in full | Show a known monetary amount with full digits and its stated currency, e.g. `$5,000`, not `$5K`. Keep the entire numeric run LTR in Arabic. Never invent an amount, currency, or precision. |
| 7 | Stay out of TikTok's way | Check the intended platform's UI and crop; keep important text clear of controls. Retain the existing safe box as a baseline, not a guarantee for every platform/device. |
| 8 | Never cover your face | Keep text and graphics clear of the eyes/mouth and facial expression throughout the beat. A deliberate full-screen cutaway is different from an accidental overlay covering the speaker. |
| 9 | Things just appear | Hooks, top text and captions appear directly, with no bounce, rise, or fade. Footage/diagram transitions remain available when useful or requested; the overview heading alone does not require removing every transition. |
| 10 | Show the real thing | Actual named apps, articles and screenshots, not decorative emoji or fabricated mock evidence. Missing asset: remain on the face and report it. |
| 11 | Files as files | Show the actual file's recognizable filename/extension or real document preview. Do not replace it with a generic decorative icon; preserve privacy when showing paths or contents. |
| 12 | Real receipts only | Use genuine source material for proof, results, receipts and testimonials. Never invent screenshots or numbers as evidence; clearly label hypothetical illustrations. |
| 13 | B-roll shows what you say | Match each available clip to its named item and spoken time; do not substitute a generic checklist. |
| 14 | Punch in on your face | Optional zooms emphasize the speaker's delivery, never zoom footage hidden behind a full-screen visual, split, or B-roll window. No automatic zoom schedule. |
| 15 | Voice on top | Keep speech intelligible above supporting audio. Music is not added automatically; the example's 25%/10% values and chosen song are not universal settings. |

The other clips cover feedback, the critique of automatic effects, and workflow/production
examples. Their notes are accounted for below; production counts and a team montage are not
editing requirements. No delegation requirement or background-music feature is inferred from them.
